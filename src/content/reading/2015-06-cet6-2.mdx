### Passage One · Moving beyond the GM crop debate

<SentenceNote number="R1-01">
  I’ll admit I’ve never quite understood the obsession surrounding genetically modified (GM) crops.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-02">
  To environmentalist opponents, GM foods are simply evil, an understudied, possibly harmful tool used by big agricultural businesses to control global seed markets and crush local farmers.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-03">
  They argue that GM foods have never delivered on their supposed promise, that money spent on GM crops would be better channeled to organic farming and that consumers should be protected with warning labels on any products that contain genetically modified ingredients.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-04">
  To supporters, GM crops are a key part of the effort to sustainably provide food to meet a growing global population.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-05">
  But more than that, supporters see the GM opposition of many environmentalists as fundamentally anti-science, no different than those who question the basics of man-made climate change.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-06">
  For both sides, GM foods seem to act as a symbol: you’re pro-agricultural business or anti-science.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-07">
  But science is exactly what we need more of when it comes to GM foods, which is why I was happy to see Nature devote a special series of articles to the GM food controversy.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-08">
  The conclusion: while GM crops haven’t yet realized their initial promise and have been dominated by agricultural businesses, there is reason to continue to use and develop them to help meet the enormous challenge of sustainably feeding a growing planet.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-09">
  That doesn’t mean GM crops are perfect, or a one-size-fits-all solution to global agriculture problems.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-10">
  But anything that can increase farming efficiency—the amount of crops we can produce per acre of land—will be extremely useful.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-11">
  GM crops can and almost certainly will be part of that suite of tools, but so will traditional plant breeding, improved soil and crop management—and perhaps most important of all, better storage and transport infrastructure, especially in the developing world.

<SentenceNote number="R1-12">

</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-13">

<SentenceNote number="R1-14">

</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R1-15">

<SentenceNote number="R1-16">

### Passage Two · The hidden costs of early decision

<SentenceNote number="R2-01">
  Early decision—you apply to one school, and admission is binding—seems like a great choice for nervous applicants.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-02">
  Schools let in a higher percentage of early-decision applicants, which arguably means that you have a better chance of getting in.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-03">
  And if you do, you’re done with the whole agonizing process by December.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-04">
  But what most students and parents don’t realize is that schools have hidden motives for offering early decision.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-05">
  Early decision, since it’s binding, allows schools to fill their classes with qualified students; it allows admissions committees to select the students that are in particular demand for their college and know those students will come.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-06">
  It also gives schools a higher yield rate, which is often used as one of the ways to measure college selectivity and popularity.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-07">
  The problem is that this process effectively shortens the window of time students have to make one of the most important decisions of their lives up to that point.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-08">
  Under regular admissions, seniors have until May 1 to choose which school to attend.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-09">
  Early decision effectively steals six months from them, months that could be used to visit more schools, do more research, speak to current students and alumni and arguably make a more informed decision.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-10">
  There are, frankly, an astonishing number of exceptional colleges in America, and for any given student, there are a number of schools that are a great fit.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-11">
  When students become too fixated on a particular school early in the admissions process, that fixation can lead to severe disappointment if they don’t get in or, if they do, the possibility that they are now bound to go to a school that, given time for further reflection, may not actually be right for them.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-12">
  Insofar as early decision offers a genuine admissions edge, that advantage goes largely to students who already have numerous advantages.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-13">
  The students who use early decision tend to be those who have received higher-quality college guidance, usually a result of coming from a more privileged background.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-14">
  In this regard, there’s an argument against early decision, as students from lower-income families are far less likely to have the admissions know-how to navigate the often confusing early deadlines.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-15">
  Students who have done their research and are confident that there’s one school they would be thrilled to get into should, under the current system, probably apply under early decision.
</SentenceNote>

<SentenceNote number="R2-16">
  But for students who haven’t yet done enough research, or who are still constantly changing their minds on favorite schools, the early-decision system needlessly and prematurely narrows the field of possibility just at a time when students should be opening themselves to a whole range of thrilling options.
</SentenceNote>
